Astronomical time

Local sidereal time (LST) is the hour angle of the vernal point; a star whose RA equals the LST is culminating right now. RAMC is the LST expressed in degrees — the basis for the MC and the house cusps.

Upcoming events

Eclipses, lunar perigee/apogee, greatest elongations of Mercury & Venus, and oppositions of the outer planets over the next 18 months.

For a global solar eclipse, “greatest eclipse” is the point on Earth where the eclipse is largest. For lunar eclipses, the “From your location” column says whether the Moon is above the horizon at maximum. The highlighted row is the next solar eclipse actually visible from your location, which may be more than 18 months away.

Aspects between two moving points are geocentric, so they happen at the same instant everywhere on Earth — no location needed. Times are shown in the time zone selected on the Calculator page.

Calculation engine: Astronomy Engine v2.1.19 (Don Cross, MIT licence) — a VSOP87 planetary model and a NOVAS-derived lunar theory, running entirely in the browser. Rise and set times use the standard 34′ refraction and the upper limb of the disc; real atmospheric conditions can shift them by 1–2 minutes.

The Asc, MC and house cusps are derived from local sidereal time and the true obliquity of the ecliptic for that date. The lunar node shown in the tables is the True Node, taken from the instantaneous angular-momentum vector of the Moon's orbit; the Mean Node is given alongside for comparison. Chiron and Lilith are not included, as they need an asteroid ephemeris this engine does not carry.

The crescent orientation in the phase icons is schematic. Time zones use a fixed offset (Indonesia observes no daylight saving).
